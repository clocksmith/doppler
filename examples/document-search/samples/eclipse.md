# Understanding Solar and Lunar Eclipses

An eclipse occurs when one astronomical body passes through the shadow of another body.

## Solar Eclipses
A solar eclipse happens when the Moon moves directly between the Sun and Earth, casting its shadow upon Earth's surface.
- **Totality**: Occurs only within the dark central umbra shadow cone, where the Sun's photosphere is completely hidden, revealing the solar corona.
- **Partial Eclipse**: Experienced in the lighter penumbra region, where the Moon covers only a portion of the solar disk.
- **Annular Eclipse**: Happens when the Moon is near apogee (farthest point in its orbit); its apparent diameter is smaller than the Sun, creating a "ring of fire".

## Eye Safety
Never look directly at the Sun during partial or annular phases without certified ISO 12312-2 solar viewing glasses or approved projection methods.

## Lunar Eclipses
A lunar eclipse occurs when Earth moves directly between the Sun and Moon, casting Earth's shadow across the lunar surface. Because Earth's atmosphere bends and scatters sunlight, red wavelengths filter through onto the eclipsed Moon, giving it a copper-red appearance often called a "blood moon".
